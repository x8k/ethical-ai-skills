# Chapter V: The Facts Stated

## Core Idea
This chapter synthesizes the evidence presented in previous chapters to establish the factual basis of women's economic dependence. It demonstrates that the condition is not natural, inevitable, or beneficial, but rather a historical development with identifiable causes and effects.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Evidence-Based Sociology**: Using observable facts rather than moral assumptions to analyze social conditions.
- **Causal Chain Analysis**: Tracing effects back to their economic causes in the sex-relation.

## Key Concepts
- **Factual Basis**: The accumulated evidence of women's subordinate economic position across societies.
- **Causal Links**: The connections between economic dependence and observed social phenomena.

## Mental Models
- **Facts over assumptions**: Social analysis must be grounded in verifiable conditions, not moral preconceptions.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring evidence**: Dismissing factual observations because they challenge traditional beliefs.

## Key Takeaways
1. Women's economic dependence is a factual, observable condition.
2. This condition produces predictable social effects.
3. The causes can be identified through systematic analysis.
4. Solutions require addressing the economic structure, not individual behavior.
