# Chapter VII: The Established Condition

## Core Idea
The sexuo-economic relation has been so long established and widespread that it appears natural and inevitable. This chapter examines how this condition became entrenched and why it persists despite its harmful effects, treating it as a historical development rather than a natural law.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Historical Entrenchment**: How long-standing social conditions become accepted as natural.
- **Path Dependence in Social Evolution**: Once a social arrangement is established, it creates its own reinforcing mechanisms.

## Key Concepts
- **Established Condition**: The sexuo-economic relation as a long-standing, widely accepted social arrangement.
- **Social Inertia**: The resistance to change in entrenched social systems.
- **Cultural Normalization**: The process by which abnormal conditions come to be seen as normal.

## Mental Models
- **Social conditions as historical artifacts**: What seems natural is often the result of historical contingencies.
- **Inertia as obstacle**: The longer a condition persists, the harder it is to change, regardless of its merits.

## Anti-patterns
- **Naturalizing history**: Assuming that long-standing conditions are natural or inevitable.

## Key Takeaways
1. The sexuo-economic relation is established, not natural.
2. Its persistence is due to historical entrenchment, not inherent rightness.
3. Social inertia maintains harmful conditions long after their causes are forgotten.
4. Cultural normalization makes abnormal conditions appear inevitable.
