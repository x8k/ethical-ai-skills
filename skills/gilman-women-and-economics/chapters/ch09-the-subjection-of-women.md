# Chapter IX: The Subjection of Women

## Core Idea
The economic dependence of women on men creates a system of subjection where women are not full agents but rather economic dependents. This chapter examines how this subjection manifests and why it persists, despite being contrary to principles of human equality and social progress.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Subjection as Economic Phenomenon**: Women's subordination is primarily economic, not biological or moral.
- **Dependence vs. Autonomy**: The contrast between women's economic dependence and the ideal of individual autonomy.

## Key Concepts
- **Economic Subjection**: The condition of being economically controlled by another.
- **Agency Limitation**: How economic dependence restricts women's ability to make free choices.
- **Structural Inequality**: The systemic nature of women's subordinate position.

## Mental Models
- **Dependence as power imbalance**: Economic subjection creates and maintains hierarchical power relations.

## Anti-patterns
- **Blame the dependent**: Holding women responsible for their subjection rather than the system that creates it.

## Key Takeaways
1. Women's subjection is fundamentally economic in nature.
2. Economic dependence limits autonomy and agency.
3. Subjection is structural, not individual.
4. Addressing subjection requires economic, not just legal or moral, changes.
