# Chapter XIV: Changing Conceptions

## Core Idea
Social change begins with changing how we conceive of gender, economics, and social relations. This chapter examines how shifting cultural, legal, and economic conceptions can pave the way for material changes in the sexuo-economic relation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Conceptual Precedence**: Changes in thinking precede changes in social structures.
- **Cultural Evolution**: The process by which social conceptions gradually transform.

## Key Concepts
- **Changing Conceptions**: Shifts in how society understands gender and economics.
- **Ideological Transformation**: Altering the beliefs and values that support existing structures.
- **Cultural Lag**: The delay between conceptual change and social transformation.

## Mental Models
- **Ideas as levers**: Changing conceptions enables structural change.
- **Culture as foundation**: Social structures rest on cultural conceptions that must be addressed.

## Anti-patterns
- **Conceptual conservatism**: Resisting new ideas that challenge existing structures.

## Key Takeaways
1. Social change requires changing how we think about gender and economics.
2. Conceptional changes precede and enable structural changes.
3. Cultural evolution is a necessary part of social transformation.
4. Addressing cultural lag accelerates social progress.
