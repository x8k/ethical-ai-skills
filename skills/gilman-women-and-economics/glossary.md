# Glossary — Women and Economics

*Knowledge base from "Women and Economics: A Study of the Economic Relation Between Men and Women as a Factor in Social Evolution" by Charlotte Perkins Gilman*

---

## A
- **Agency Limitation** — The restriction of women's ability to make free choices due to economic dependence on men (Ch 9)
- **Artificial Division** — Economic roles divided by sex rather than by aptitude, interest, or efficiency (Ch 11)

## C
- **Care Work as Infrastructure** — The foundation of reproductive labor upon which all other economic activity depends (Ch 10)
- **Changing Conceptions** — Shifts in how society understands gender and economics, preceding structural change (Ch 14)
- **Children as Social Capital** — The recognition that investing in maternity is investing in society's future (Ch 10)
- **Conceptual Conservatism** — Resisting new ideas that challenge existing social structures (Ch 14, Anti-pattern)
- **Conceptual Precedence** — The principle that changes in thinking must precede changes in social structures (Ch 14)
- **Cultural Evolution** — The gradual transformation of social conceptions and values (Ch 14)
- **Cultural Lag** — The delay between conceptual change and social transformation (Ch 14)
- **Cultural Normalization** — The process by which abnormal conditions come to be seen as natural and inevitable (Ch 7)

## D
- **Dependence as Power Imbalance** — How economic subjection creates and maintains hierarchical power relations between sexes (Ch 9)
- **Dependence vs. Autonomy** — The fundamental contrast between women's economic dependence and the ideal of individual autonomy (Ch 9)
- **Domestic Economy** — The sphere of economic activity traditionally assigned to women, including household management and care work (Ch 6)

## E
- **Economic Dependence as Structural** — Women's reliance on men for livelihood as a systematic, societal condition, not individual choice (Ch 4)
- **Economic Interdependence** — How male and female economic roles reinforce each other within the sexuo-economic system (Ch 6)
- **Economic Subjection** — The condition of women being economically controlled by men (Ch 9)
- **Emerging Conditions** — New social and economic factors (industrialization, education) that make change in sexuo-economic relations possible (Ch 8)
- **Equitable Economic Participation** — Creating economic systems that enable full participation by all members of society (Ch 13)
- **Established Condition** — The sexuo-economic relation as a long-standing, widely accepted social arrangement (Ch 7)
- **Evolutionary Trend** — The natural progression toward optimal social arrangements, which economic conditions can distort (Ch 2)
- **Excessive Sex-Distinction** — The unprecedented degree of differentiation between males and females in humans, extending beyond biology into social, psychological, and moral realms (Ch 3)

## F
- **Factual Basis** — The accumulated, observable evidence of women's subordinate economic position across societies (Ch 5)
- **Functional Specialization by Sex** — The assignment of different economic and social functions to males and females (Ch 6)
- **Function-Organ Development** — The principle that "functions and organs are developed by use"—daily activities most modify human structure and function (Ch 1)

## H
- **Hidden Economic Cause** — The underlying, continuous economic condition that operates against the natural trend toward healthier sex-relations (Ch 2)
- **Holistic Solution** — Addressing economic, social, and cultural dimensions together to transform the sexuo-economic relation (Ch 15)

## I
- **Ideological Transformation** — Altering the beliefs and values that support existing social structures (Ch 14)
- **Invisible Economy** — Women's uncounted and unvalued economic contributions, particularly domestic and reproductive labor (Ch 6)

## M
- **Maladjustment** — The current sexuo-economic relation as a maladaptation that can be corrected through understanding and action (Ch 8)
- **Maternal Passion** — A sex-distinction attribute, as valid as physical traits like a lion's mane (Ch 3)
- **Monogamous Marriage** — The form of sex-union that social evolution has determined is best for individuals, offspring, and society; a natural development, not an artificial imposition (Ch 2)
- **Morbid Action** — The unnatural, pathological aspects of human sex-relation that produce evil results (Ch 2)

## N
- **Natural vs. Unnatural in Sex-Relation** — The distinction between sex relations that follow evolutionary trends (natural) and those distorted by economic conditions (unnatural) (Ch 2)

## O
- **Over-Sexed** — Exhibiting excessive sex-distinctions, whether in physical traits or behaviors, to a degree that harms the individual or species (Ch 3)

## P
- **Path Dependence in Social Evolution** — The phenomenon by which once a social arrangement is established, it creates its own reinforcing mechanisms, making change difficult (Ch 7)
- **Peculiar Combination of Functions** — The unique way women's economic position combines domestic, reproductive, and social functions (Ch 6)
- **Primary Sex-Distinctions** — Essential reproductive organs and functions that differ between males and females (Ch 3)
- **Progressive Evolution** — Social change as a deliberate, positive development toward greater equity and efficiency (Ch 15)

## R
- **Race-Modification** — How economic conditions create distinct racial characteristics (e.g., the Hebrew people under commercial restrictions) (Ch 1)
- **Reconstructed Positions** — New social and economic roles for men and women, designed to eliminate artificial divisions (Ch 13)
- **Reproductive Labor** — The unpaid work of bearing and raising children, economically valued differently from male labor (Ch 6, 10)
- **Reproductive Labor as Economic** — The principle that maternity work should be economically valued and compensated (Ch 10)

## S
- **Sex-Distinction** — Any trait, behavior, or characteristic that differs between males and females, whether primary (biological) or secondary (social) (Ch 3)
- **Sex-Economic Combination** — The intertwining of reproductive relations with economic relations in human society (Ch 4)
- **Sex-Energy** — The primal manifestation of reproductive drive, which in human males exceeds reproductive needs, causing social harm (Ch 3)
- **Sexuo-Economic Analysis** — The comprehensive framework for understanding gender relations through an economic lens (Ch 15)
- **Sexuo-Economic Relation** — The unique human condition where "the sex-relation is also an economic relation" and "an entire sex lives in a relation of economic dependence upon the other sex" (Ch 1, 4)
- **Social Equity** — Fair distribution of economic opportunities and responsibilities between sexes (Ch 13)
- **Social Environment** — The organic interchanges constituting social life, affecting individuals more profoundly than physical conditions (Ch 1)
- **Social Inertia** — The resistance to change in entrenched social systems (Ch 7)
- **Social Investment** — Children as the future of society, making maternity work socially essential (Ch 10)
- **Social Reconstruction** — The process of redesigning social and economic roles to eliminate artificial divisions (Ch 13)
- **Social Responsibility** — The obligation to improve social conditions once problems are recognized (Ch 12)
- **Social Waste** — The loss of potential when half the population is restricted in economic participation due to sex-based divisions (Ch 11)
- **Sociological Analysis of Sex Phenomena** — The scientific study of sex-related behaviors, both normal and abnormal, as natural phenomena (Ch 2)
- **Structural Inequality** — The systemic nature of women's subordinate economic position (Ch 9)
- **Subjection as Economic Phenomenon** — Women's subordination as primarily economic in nature, not biological or moral (Ch 9)

## T
- **Social Transformation** — The process of changing social structures through understanding and action (Ch 15)

## V
- **Virtues as Sex-Differentiated** — The assignment of different moral qualities to men and women in human society, revealing the depth of sex-distinction (Ch 3)
