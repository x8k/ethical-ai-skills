# Preface

**Stephanus Pages**: 4:387-392

## Core Idea
Kant sets the stage for the Groundwork by explaining the need for a metaphysics of morals, distinguishing it from empirical ethics, and outlining the structure of the work. He argues that pure philosophy must be separated from empirical philosophy, and that metaphysics of morals is necessary to establish the supreme principle of morality.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Division of Philosophy
**When to use**: When classifying different types of philosophical inquiry or understanding Kant's organizational scheme.

**Structure**:
```
PHILOSOPHY
├── Material (concerned with objects)
│   ├── Physics (laws of nature)
│   │   ├── Empirical
│   │   └── Rational (Metaphysics of Nature)
│   └── Ethics (laws of freedom)
│       ├── Empirical (Practical Anthropology)
│       └── Rational (Metaphysics of Morals)
└── Formal (concerned with form of understanding/reason)
    ├── Logic (rules of thinking)
    └── (No empirical part - logic is purely a priori)
```

**Key Distinction**:
- **Material Philosophy**: Deals with determinate objects and the laws they follow
- **Formal Philosophy**: Deals with the form of understanding and reason itself
- **Empirical vs. Pure**: Empirical is based on experience; pure is based on a priori principles

**Mental Model**: Think of philosophy as a tree:
- Trunk = Formal philosophy (logic)
- Two main branches = Material philosophy (physics and ethics)
- Each branch has empirical and pure (rational) parts

### The Necessity of Metaphysics of Morals
**When to use**: When defending the need for a priori moral philosophy or explaining why empirical ethics is insufficient.

**Argument**:
1. All rational cognition is either material or formal
2. Material philosophy that deals with laws of freedom (ethics) must have both empirical and rational parts
3. The empirical part is practical anthropology (how humans actually act)
4. The rational part is metaphysics of morals (what should be done)
5. **Without metaphysics of morals**, we have no pure moral philosophy, only descriptions of how people happen to act

**Analogy**: Just as physics needs metaphysics of nature to establish its universal principles, ethics needs metaphysics of morals.

### The Division of Labor in Philosophy
**When to use**: When discussing specialization in academic disciplines or the proper method for philosophy.

**Argument**:
1. All trades and arts benefit from division of labor
2. Philosophy is a "learned trade" that also benefits from division
3. Those who mix empirical and rational philosophy without distinction are like bunglers trying to do two jobs at once
4. **Solution**: Carefully separate the empirical part from the rational part

**Moral**: Just as we wouldn't want a doctor who doesn't understand the difference between anatomy and physiology, we shouldn't want a philosopher who confuses empirical and rational principles.

## Key Concepts

### Ancient Greek Philosophy Division
- **Physics**: Study of nature
- **Ethics**: Study of morals
- **Logic**: Study of thinking
- **Kant's addition**: We need the principle that unifies these and allows proper subdivision

### Two Parts of Material Philosophy
- **Physics**: Laws of nature (what is)
- **Ethics**: Laws of freedom (what ought to be)

### Empirical vs. Pure Philosophy
- **Empirical Philosophy**: Based on grounds of experience
- **Pure Philosophy**: Based on a priori principles
- **Note**: Logic can only be pure (no empirical logic possible)

### Metaphysics of Morals
- **Definition**: The rational part of ethics (as opposed to practical anthropology, the empirical part)
- **Purpose**: To establish the supreme principle of morality
- **Method**: A priori, not based on experience

### Metaphysics of Nature
- **Definition**: The rational part of physics
- **Purpose**: To establish universal principles of nature
- **Relation to Metaphysics of Morals**: Both are needed; the Groundwork focuses on the latter

## Mental Models

- **Use the Tree of Philosophy**: When organizing philosophical knowledge, use the division: Formal (logic) vs. Material (physics/ethics), each with empirical and pure parts.
- **Use the Division of Labor Principle**: When studying philosophy, specialize in either empirical or rational parts, not both mixed together.
- **Use the Metaphysics Bridge**: When moving from description to prescription, use metaphysics as the bridge between what is and what ought to be.

## Anti-patterns

- **Philosophical Bungling**: Mixing empirical and rational philosophy without careful distinction
- **Empirical Reductionism**: Trying to reduce all ethics to practical anthropology (describing how people act)
- **Metaphysical Neglect**: Studying ethics without a foundation in metaphysics of morals

## Key Takeaways

1. Philosophy is divided into material (physics, ethics) and formal (logic) parts
2. Each material part has empirical and rational subdivisions
3. Metaphysics of morals is the rational part of ethics, establishing a priori moral principles
4. Practical anthropology is the empirical part of ethics, describing how humans actually act
5. The Groundwork aims to establish the supreme principle of morality (the categorical imperative)
6. Pure philosophy must be carefully separated from empirical philosophy
7. Division of labor applies to philosophy as to other trades
8. Kant's work fills the gap in the ancient division by providing the principle for metaphysics of morals
9. The ultimate goal is a complete metaphysics of morals, which the Groundwork prepares for
10. This work is the foundation — the Critique of Practical Reason and Metaphysics of Morals will build upon it

## Connects To

- **Section I**: Begins the project of establishing metaphysics of morals with the analysis of the good will
- **Kant's other works**: The Critique of Pure Reason provides the theoretical foundation; the Critique of Practical Reason and Metaphysics of Morals complete the practical philosophy
- **Ancient philosophy**: Extends the Greek division of philosophy (physics, ethics, logic) by adding the metaphysics foundation
- **Modern philosophy**: Anticipates the analytic philosophy distinction between normative and descriptive ethics
