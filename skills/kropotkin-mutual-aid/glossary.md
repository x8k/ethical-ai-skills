# Glossary - Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution

**Anarchism** — Political philosophy advocating stateless societies based on voluntary associations and mutual aid (Ch 00, Ch 07)

**Anarchist-Communism** — Form of anarchism advocating common ownership of the means of production and direct democracy, theoretically grounded in mutual aid principles (Ch 07)

**Artel** — Russian form of co-operative labor association; examples cited by Kropotkin as evidence of mutual aid in modern society (Ch 06)

**Barbarians** — Kropotkin's term for early Germanic tribes and similar societies, characterized by village communities and mutual support networks (Ch 04)

**Broad Struggle for Existence** — Darwin's original conception including dependence of one being on another, success in leaving progeny, and environmental adaptation (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Civilization** — For Kropotkin, not synonymous with progress; often suppresses mutual aid instincts through hierarchical institutions (Ch 09)

**Co-operation** — Voluntary joint action for mutual benefit; the practical expression of mutual aid (Ch 01, Ch 05)

**Colony-Stages** — Early evolutionary stages where sociability proper first emerges, predating complex parental feelings (Ch 00)

**Communism** — Social system based on common ownership and mutual support; distinguished from state socialism (Ch 07)

**Competition** — Biological and social struggle between individuals; contrasted with mutual aid as equally fundamental but less evolutionarily significant (Ch 01)

**Darwinism** — Theory of evolution by natural selection; Kropotkin critiques its narrow interpretation while accepting its broad principles (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Descent of Man** — Darwin's work containing observations on mutual support in animal societies, later developed by Kropotkin (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Evolution** — Process of gradual development; for Kropotkin, driven as much by mutual aid as by competition (Ch 00, Ch 01, Ch 03)

**Folkmote** — Medieval assembly of all householders; early democratic institution based on mutual aid principles (Ch 04, Ch 05)

**Guild** — Medieval association of craftsmen; combined economic regulation with mutual support (Ch 05, Ch 06)

**Huxleyan Darwinism** — Narrow interpretation of Darwinism emphasizing perpetual struggle and competition, named after Thomas Huxley (Ch 01)

**Kessler's Law** — The law of Mutual Aid, articulated by Professor Kessler, stating that mutual aid is far more essential for evolution than mutual struggle (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Law of Mutual Aid** — Natural law stating that co-operation and mutual support are fundamental to species survival and evolution (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Law of Mutual Struggle** — Natural law of competition and struggle; contrasted with the law of Mutual Aid (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Malthusianism** — Theory that population growth outstrips food supply, leading to inevitable competition; critiqued by Kropotkin for ignoring mutual aid (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Medieval City** — Urban center characterized by guilds, folkmotes, and extensive mutual support networks (Ch 04, Ch 05, Ch 06)

**Mutual Aid** — Co-operative behavior among animals or humans that increases survival chances and species development (Ch 00, Ch 01-08)

**Mutual Support** — Practical assistance and solidarity between members of a group or species (Ch 00, Ch 01-08)

**Natural Checks** — Environmental factors limiting population growth (climate, food scarcity, disease); more significant than intra-species competition (Ch 00)

**Natural Selection** — Darwin's mechanism of evolution; Kropotkin argues it operates through mutual aid as much as through competition (Ch 01)

**Narrow Struggle for Existence** — Reduction of Darwin's concept to mere individual competition for means of subsistence (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Paucity of Life** — Under-population characteristic of regions like Northern Asia; results from natural checks (Ch 00)

**Progressive Evolution** — Evolution that increases species intelligence, organization, and welfare; facilitated by mutual aid (Ch 00, Ch 01)

**Property** — For Proudhon and Kropotkin, an institution that contradicts mutual aid principles; "Property is Theft" (referenced in Ch 07)

**Savages** — Kropotkin's term for early human societies, characterized by primitive communism and mutual aid (Ch 03)

**Socialism** — Economic and social system based on social ownership; Kropotkin distinguishes his anarchist-communism from state socialism (Ch 07)

**Sociability** — Tendency of animals to form social groups and support each other; as much a law of nature as mutual struggle (Ch 00, Ch 01, Ch 02)

**State** — Hierarchical institution that suppresses mutual aid instincts and imposes artificial competition (Ch 09)

**Survival of the Fittest** — Darwinian phrase co-opted by social Darwinists; Kropotkin redefines the fittest as those who co-operate, not those who compete (Ch 01)

**Sympathy** — Emotional capacity for mutual understanding; Darwin argued that communities with the most sympathetic members flourish best (Ch 01)

**Village Community** — Early agricultural settlement characterized by communal land ownership and mutual support; basis of medieval institutions (Ch 04)
