# Chapter II: Marriage as Institution of Subjection

## Core Idea
Marriage, as currently structured by law and custom, is not a contract between equals but an institution that legally subordinates women to men. The conditions annexed to marriage make women's position one of dependency and inequality rather than partnership.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Marriage Contract Analysis**: Examining marriage laws to reveal how they create and maintain subjection.
- **Legal Subordination in Marriage**: The legal framework that makes wives subject to their husbands' authority.
- **Domestic Tyranny**: The potential for abuse of power within the family when one party is legally superior.

## Key Concepts
- **Marriage as Destination**: Marriage is presented by society as women's ultimate goal and proper sphere.
- **Forced Choice**: Women are brought up with marriage as their only socially acceptable option.
- **Legal Inequality**: Laws make wives legally subject to their husbands in matters of property, children, and personal freedom.
- **Property Rights**: Married women's property typically becomes their husband's under the law.
- **Divorce Inequality**: Women have far fewer rights than men to dissolve a marriage.

## Mental Models
- **Institution vs. contract**: Marriage is often an institution of subjection, not a free contract between equals.
- **Law as teacher**: Laws shape social expectations and behaviors by defining what is normal and acceptable.
- **Power creates abuse**: When one party has legal power over another, abuse of that power is likely.

## Anti-patterns
- **Romanticizing subjection**: Portraying women's dependence as natural or desirable.
- **False equality**: Claiming marriage is equal when laws and customs create inequality.
- **Victim blaming**: Blaming women for the consequences of their legally inferior position.

## Key Takeaways
1. Marriage laws create legal subordination of women to men.
2. Women are socialized to accept marriage as their only destination.
3. The legal conditions of marriage make it often undesirable for women.
4. When laws give one party power over another, that power is frequently abused.
5. True marriage reform requires making marriage a partnership of equals.
