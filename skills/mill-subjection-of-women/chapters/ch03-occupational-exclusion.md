# Chapter III: Occupational Exclusion and Political Rights

## Core Idea
Excluding women from the functions and occupations historically monopolized by men is both unjust and a means of maintaining their domestic subordination. The primary reason for this exclusion is that men "cannot yet tolerate the idea of living with an equal."

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Occupational Monopoly**: The historical monopolization of certain functions by the "stronger sex."
- **Functional Equality**: The principle that qualifications, not sex, should determine who performs which functions.
- **Social Barriers**: The customs and laws that prevent women from entering certain occupations.

## Key Concepts
- **Monopoly of Functions**: Men's historical exclusion of women from certain occupations to maintain their own privileges.
- **Admissibility to Functions**: Women's right to be considered for all occupations based on their abilities.
- **Competitive Advantage**: The benefits to society of removing artificial barriers to competition.
- **Moral Character**: The claim that women's supposed moral character (purity, modesty) would be compromised by certain occupations.
- **Physical Limitations**: The argument that women's physical strength makes them unsuited for certain jobs.

## Mental Models
- **Exclusion as control**: Occupational exclusion maintains women's dependence on men.
- **Capability as emerging**: True abilities can only be known when people are free to try.
- **Monopoly as inefficient**: Artificial restrictions reduce overall social productivity.

## Anti-patterns
- **Circular reasoning**: "Women can't do X because they've never done X."
- **Moving goalposts**: When women succeed in one area, claiming that area wasn't "really" the male domain.
- **Essentializing differences**: Assuming biological differences justify all social divisions.

## Key Takeaways
1. Occupational exclusion is primarily a means of maintaining women's domestic subordination.
2. Men's resistance to women's equality stems from discomfort with living as equals.
3. The justice of admitting women to all occupations is clear from principles of equality.
4. Artificial barriers reduce social efficiency and progress.
5. True capabilities can only be known when people are free to attempt various occupations.
