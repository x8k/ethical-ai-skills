# Cheatsheet — The Subjection of Women

*Quick reference for Mill's frameworks and arguments*

---

## 🎯 Core Frameworks at a Glance

| Framework | Key Insight | Test | Application |
|-----------|-------------|------|-------------|
| **Perfect Equality** | Only just principle: no power/privilege/disability by sex | Does it create sex-based inequality? | Any gender-based law or custom |
| **Hindrance to Improvement** | Women's subjection prevents social progress | Does this hinder human development? | All gender inequalities |
| **Custom vs. Principle** | Traditions must yield to rational principles | Does custom conflict with principle? | Traditional gender roles |
| **Power Creates Abuse** | Power imbalances lead to abuse | Who has power? Who is harmed? | Marriage, employment hierarchy |
| **True Nature Discovery** | Current "nature" is artificial; true nature emerges under freedom | Are traits shaped by conditions? | Gender differences |
| **Utilitarian Case** | Equality benefits all of society | Who benefits? | Any reform proposal |
| **Harm Principle** | Only harm to others justifies restriction | Who is harmed? | Any proposed restriction |

## ⚖️ Evaluation Tests

### Is this social arrangement just?
```
Social Arrangement?
├── Does it create sex-based power/privilege/disability?
│   ├── YES → Violates Perfect Equality → Unjust
│   └── NO → Proceed to next test
├── Does it prevent human improvement?
│   ├── YES → Hindrance to Progress → Unjust
│   └── NO → Proceed to next test
└── Does it conflict with rational principles?
    ├── YES → Custom vs. Principle → Unjust
    └── NO → Potentially just
```

### Should this restriction on women be removed?
```
Restriction?
├── Does it prevent harm to others?
│   ├── NO → Unjustified by Harm Principle → Should be removed
│   └── YES → Is harm significant and likely?
│       ├── NO → Should be removed
│       └── YES → Is there a less restrictive alternative?
│           ├── YES → Replace with alternative
│           └── NO → May be justified (rare)
└── Who benefits from the restriction?
    ├── Men only → Suspect motivation → Should be removed
    └── Society/everyone → Re-evaluate
```

## 💡 Quick Responses to Common Claims

### "But it's tradition!"
**Response**: "The fact that a custom has existed is no presumption of its goodness, when any other sufficient cause can be assigned for its existence. Women's subjection, like slavery, was maintained by force. That it has been customary does not make it just."

### "Women are naturally different!"
**Response**: "What is now called the nature of women is an eminently artificial thing—the result of forced repression in some directions, unnatural stimulation in others. We cannot know women's true nature until they are free to develop without artificial constraints."

### "But what about the children?"
**Response**: "If there is any one function for which women have shown a decided vocation, it is that of reigning [or governing]. The claim that women must be excluded from public life because of maternity applies only to mothers, not to all women. Individual choice should determine priorities."

### "Men and women are just different!"
**Response**: "Even if there are differences, the principle of perfect equality admits no power or privilege on one side, nor disability on the other. Differences of capability, if they exist, would emerge naturally under freedom and would not justify legal inequality."

### "It would disrupt society!"
**Response**: "Women's subjection is itself a chief hindrance to human improvement. Freeing women would double society's mental resources and accelerate progress. The disruption of ending an injustice is the path to a better society."

## 📋 Reform Design Checklist

When designing social reforms for gender equality:

- [ ] Removes all sex-based power, privilege, or disability
- [ ] Creates conditions for perfect equality
- [ ] Enables full development of human capabilities
- [ ] Accelerates human improvement
- [ ] Passes the Harm Principle test (no restriction without harm to others)
- [ ] Benefits all of society, not just women
- [ ] Is based on rational principles, not custom
- [ ] Allows for discovery of true nature under freedom

## 🎯 Argument Structure Templates

### For Equality in Any Domain
```
Premise 1: The only just principle for human relations is perfect equality
Premise 2: [Current arrangement] creates sex-based power/privilege/disability
Conclusion: [Current arrangement] is unjust and should be reformed

Supporting points:
- It hinders human improvement
- It conflicts with rational principles
- Custom does not justify it
- Removing it would benefit society
```

### Against Occupational Exclusion
```
Premise 1: Occupations should be open to those with qualifications
Premise 2: Sex is not a qualification for any occupation
Premise 3: Current exclusion is based on sex, not qualifications
Conclusion: Occupational exclusion based on sex is unjust

Supporting evidence:
- Women have succeeded when allowed to try
- Exclusion maintains women's dependence
- Society loses valuable contributions
```

### For Marriage Reform
```
Premise 1: Marriage should be a partnership of equals
Premise 2: Current marriage laws create legal subordination of women
Premise 3: Power imbalances in marriage lead to abuse
Conclusion: Marriage laws should be reformed to create equality

Supporting evidence:
- Historical examples of abused power
- Benefits of equal partnership
- Principle of perfect equality
```

## 📚 Essential Quotations

> "The principle which regulates the existing social relations between the two sexes—the legal subordination of one sex to the other—is wrong in itself, and now one of the chief hindrances to human improvement."

> "The only just principle... is perfect equality, admitting no power or privilege on the one side, nor disability on the other."

> "What is now called the nature of women is an eminently artificial thing—the result of forced repression in some directions, unnatural stimulation in others."

> "The legal subordination of one sex to the other is wrong in itself, and... it ought to be replaced by a principle of perfect equality."

> "It is a political, social, and economical mistake... to keep half the human race in a state of such subjection."

> "Power tends to corrupt, and absolute power corrupts absolutely." (applied to domestic relations)
