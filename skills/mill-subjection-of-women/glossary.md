# Glossary — The Subjection of Women

*Knowledge base from "The Subjection of Women" by John Stuart Mill (1869)*

---

## A
- **Admissibility to Functions** — Women's right to be considered for all occupations and social functions based on ability, not sex (Ch 3)
- **Artificial Nature** — The current characteristics of women, which are "an eminently artificial thing—the result of forced repression in some directions, unnatural stimulation in others" (Ch 4)

## C
- **Character Development** — The ways in which freedom enables the full development of human character and capabilities (Ch 4)
- **Custom as Tyrant** — The idea that tradition and custom often function as unjust rulers over human relations (Ch 1)

## D
- **Domestic Tyranny** — The potential for abuse of power within the family when one party (the husband) is legally superior to the other (Ch 2)

## E
- **Education Benefits** — The improvements in education for both sexes that would result from women's freedom (Ch 4)
- **Equality Principle** — The principle that the only just basis for relations between sexes is perfect equality, with no power, privilege, or disability based on sex (Ch 1)

## F
- **False Equality** — The pretense that marriage is equal when laws and customs create inequality (Ch 2, Anti-pattern)
- **Forced Choice** — Women being socialized to view marriage as their only acceptable option (Ch 2)
- **Functional Equality** — The principle that qualifications, not sex, should determine who performs which functions (Ch 3)

## H
- **Harm Principle Applied to Gender** — The extension of Mill's harm principle: the only justification for restricting liberty is to prevent harm to others; custom or tradition are not valid reasons (Ch 1)
- **Hindrance to Human Improvement** — Women's subjection as a major obstacle preventing social progress and human development (Ch 1)
- **Human Improvement** — The advancement of society in moral, intellectual, and social dimensions (Ch 1, 4)

## I
- **Institution vs. Contract** — The distinction between marriage as an institution of subjection (current) and as a free contract between equals (ideal) (Ch 2)

## L
- **Legal Inequality** — Laws that make wives legally subject to their husbands in matters of property, children, and personal freedom (Ch 2)
- **Legal Subordination** — The legal system that places women under the authority of men (Ch 1)

## M
- **Marital Improvement** — The transformation of marriage from a relationship of dominion to one of true partnership that would result from equality (Ch 4)
- **Marriage as Destination** — Marriage presented by society as women's ultimate goal and proper sphere (Ch 2)
- **Monopoly of Functions** — Men's historical exclusion of women from certain occupations to maintain their own privileges (Ch 3)

## O
- **Occupational Exclusion** — The exclusion of women from functions and occupations historically monopolized by men (Ch 3)
- **Occupational Monopoly** — The historical monopolization of certain functions by the "stronger sex" (Ch 3)

## P
- **Perfect Equality** — The principle of absolute equality between sexes, without exceptions or qualifications (Ch 1)
- **Power Creates Abuse** — The principle that when one party has legal power over another, abuse of that power is likely (Ch 2)
- **Progressive Opinion** — The idea that the opinion favoring women's equality has grown stronger with reflection and experience (Ch 1)
- **Property Rights** — The legal framework by which married women's property typically becomes their husband's (Ch 2)

## R
- **Relationship as Partnership** — The ideal of marriage as a relationship of genuine mutual respect and cooperation between equals (Ch 4)
- **Resources as Capabilities** — The view of women's minds as resources currently underutilized due to subjection (Ch 4)

## S
- **Social Barriers** — The customs and laws that prevent women from entering certain occupations (Ch 3)
- **Social Improvement** — The broad advancement of society in all dimensions, which would be accelerated by women's freedom (Ch 4)
- **Social Progress** — The idea that societies can and should improve over time (Ch 1)
- **Subjection as Wrong** — The fundamental claim that the legal subordination of one sex to the other is wrong in itself (Ch 1)

## T
- **True Nature** — The characteristics women would develop under conditions of freedom and equality (Ch 4)

## U
- **Utilitarian Case for Equality** — The practical benefits of women's freedom for all of society (Ch 4)

## W
- **Women's Nature** — The current characteristics of women, largely shaped by their social condition rather than biology (Ch 4)
