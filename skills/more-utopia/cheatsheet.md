## 💡 Key Decision Guides

### Should a society abolish private property?
```
IF: Society experiences high levels of:
  ✓ Theft and crime
  ✓ Poverty and inequality
  ✓ Political corruption
  ✓ Social conflict
  ✓ Materialism and greed

THEN: Consider that private property may be the root cause

ALTERNATIVE: Common property system where:
  ✓ Goods are held in common
  ✓ Distribution is according to need
  ✓ Money is abolished
  ✓ All able-bodied citizens work
  ✓ Surplus is stored and shared

EXPECTED RESULTS:
  ✓ Elimination of poverty
  ✓ End of theft (nothing to steal)
  ✓ Reduced social conflict
  ✓ More focus on intrinsic values
  ✓ Greater social cohesion
```

### Should philosophers serve princes?
```
CONSIDER THREE OPTIONS:

1. WITHDRAW (Hythloday's initial position)
   Pros: Maintain philosophical purity
   Cons: Abandons society, selfish, cowardly

2. SERVE (More's position)
   Pros: Duty to society, potential for gradual reform
   Cons: Risk of corruption, counsel may be ignored

3. ENGAGED REFORMED (Synthesis)
   Pros: Serve while maintaining integrity
   Cons: Difficult to balance, slow progress

DECISION FACTORS:
  - How corrupt is the court?
  - Can you maintain your principles?
  - Is gradual reform possible?
  - What are the alternatives?
```

### Is a policy just?
```
USE THE UTOPIAN TEST:
1. Does it promote the common good?
2. Does it create or reduce inequality?
3. Does it address root causes or just symptoms?
4. Would it still be necessary in a society without private property?
5. Does it treat all people with dignity?

IF: Policy punishes symptoms without addressing causes
THEN: It may be unjust (e.g., punishing thieves without addressing poverty)

IF: Policy promotes equality and common good
THEN: It aligns with Utopian principles
```

## 🎯 Utopia in One Page - Core Principles

### THE UTOPIAN VISION
- **Economy**: Communism without money, with common property and distribution according to need
- **Politics**: Representative democracy with elected officials and limited executive power
- **Society**: Social equality, universal labor, rational organization
- **Religion**: Tolerance with shared moral principles
- **Justice**: Reform-focused, with slavery as temporary punishment
- **Education**: Universal, lifelong, practical
- **Healthcare**: Free and available to all
- **Urban Design**: Rational, uniform, healthy cities
- **Foreign Policy**: Peaceful but prepared for defensive war

### THE CRITIQUE (Book I)
Private property is the root cause of:
- Theft and crime
- Poverty and inequality
- Political corruption
- Social conflict
- Materialism

### THE ALTERNATIVE (Book II)
A society organized by reason can achieve:
- Prosperity for all
- Social harmony
- Individual freedom
- Moral virtue
- Human dignity

### THE CHALLENGE
Utopia = "no place" (ou-topos)
The ideal society may be unattainable, but the vision inspires reform.

**Final Question**: If Utopia is impossible, what elements can we incorporate into our own societies?
