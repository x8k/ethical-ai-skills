## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Property as Theft
**Use when**: Analyzing the nature and justification of property relations
- **Core insight**: Property in its current form is an institution that allows individuals to appropriate the collective product of society as their exclusive domain
- **Formulation**: "Property is theft!" — the individual appropriation of social value
- **Mechanism**: Property owners claim the products of collective labor (past and present) as their exclusive right
- **Application**: Examine any property claim by tracing the collective contributions that made it possible

### Mutualism
**Use when**: Designing alternative economic systems based on reciprocity and justice
- **Core insight**: Economic relations should be based on mutual exchange of equivalent values, not exploitation
- **Key principle**: "Mutuality" — the principle that individuals and groups should exchange services and products of equal value
- **Institutions**: Mutual banks, credit associations, worker co-operatives as alternatives to capitalist exploitation
- **Application**: Create economic institutions that eliminate profit, interest, and rent by ensuring equivalent exchange

### The Federative Principle
**Use when**: Designing political organization without centralized authority
- **Core insight**: Political power should be organized from the bottom up through voluntary federation, not from the top down through hierarchy
- **Structure**: Communes federate with other communes, maintaining autonomy while co-ordinating on shared concerns
- **Mechanism**: Delegates are elected with specific mandates and are recallable at any time
- **Application**: Replace state structures with federal systems of self-governing communes

### System of Economic Contradictions
**Use when**: Analyzing the inherent contradictions of capitalist economic relations
- **Core insight**: Capitalism contains internal contradictions that create instability, inequality, and crisis
- **Key contradictions**: Value vs. use-value, liberty vs. authority, competition vs. monopoly, property vs. community
- **Result**: These contradictions drive economic evolution but also create suffering that requires resolution
- **Application**: Identify and address economic contradictions through institutional reform based on mutualism

### Property is Impossible
**Use when**: Deconstructing the philosophical foundations of property rights
- **Core insight**: Property cannot be justified on any consistent moral or logical grounds
- **Arguments against property**:
  - Occupation cannot create a legitimate title
  - Labor cannot appropriate natural wealth
  - Civil law is a sanction of theft, not its prevention
  - Universal consent does not exist and cannot justify property
  - Prescription (time) does not create moral right
- **Conclusion**: Property is an institution without foundation, maintained by force and convention

### Justice as the Synthetic Principle
**Use when**: Resolving social and economic conflicts through equitable solutions
- **Core insight**: Justice is not a compromise between opposing interests but a synthetic resolution that satisfies all legitimate claims
- **Mechanism**: In each economic contradiction, identify the legitimate elements on both sides and synthesize them into a just institution
- **Application**: Replace exploitative relations with just exchanges that recognize all contributions

### Critique of Authority
**Use when**: Analyzing power relations in society
- **Core insight**: All authority, whether political, economic, or religious, represents the imposition of one will on another
- **Formulation**: "To be governed is to be kept in sight, inspected, spied upon, directed, legislated at, regulated, docketed, indoctrinated, preached at, controlled, assessed, weighed, censored, ordered about"
- **Implication**: Authority is inherently degrading and should be replaced with free association
- **Application**: Replace hierarchical authority with voluntary co-operation and federal organization

### The Coming Revolution
**Use when**: Understanding social transformation and the path to anarchist society
- **Core insight**: The 1848 revolution demonstrated that the old order was collapsing, but the work of creating a new society remained incomplete
- **Analysis**: Revolutions occur when the contradictions of the existing system become unbearable
- **Path forward**: Build alternative institutions (mutual banks, co-operatives, federations) that embody the new principles
- **Application**: Support and participate in the creation of liberatory institutions rather than waiting for a single revolutionary moment
