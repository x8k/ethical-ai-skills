# What Is Property? - Chapter 1: Method Pursued in This Work

## Core Idea
Proudhon establishes his dialectical method for analyzing property: identifying and resolving contradictions in economic and social phenomena. He promises to demonstrate that property cannot be justified on any consistent moral, logical, or economic grounds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Dialectical Method**: Proudhon's approach of identifying contradictions in existing institutions and proposing synthetic solutions
- **Contradiction Resolution**: The process of exposing the internal inconsistencies of property as an institution

## Key Concepts
- **Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis**: Proudhon's adaptation of Hegelian dialectic to social analysis
- **Property as Problem**: Property is presented as a social contradiction requiring resolution
- **Logical Refutation**: Property will be shown to be impossible to justify rationally
- **Historical Analysis**: Property will be examined through its historical development and consequences

## Mental Models
- **Use contradiction to expose truth**: Think of social analysis as identifying and resolving internal inconsistencies rather than accepting surface appearances
- **Property as unsolvable puzzle**: Prefer understanding property as a problem to be dissolved, not as a natural right to be defended

## Anti-patterns
- **Accepting surface justifications**: Taking property's claimed foundations (labor, occupation, law) at face value without examining their internal contradictions
- **Static analysis**: Treating property as a fixed institution rather than as a historically developed and contradictory phenomenon

## Key Takeaways
1. Property cannot be justified on any consistent grounds
2. The dialectical method exposes internal contradictions in social institutions
3. Logical analysis reveals property's impossibility as a moral institution
4. Historical examination shows property's harmful social consequences
5. A synthetic solution to the property contradiction must be found
