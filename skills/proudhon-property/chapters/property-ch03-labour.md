# What Is Property? - Chapter 3: Labour as the Efficient Cause of Property

## Core Idea
Proudhon refutes the claim that labor creates a legitimate title to property, demonstrating that individual labor cannot justify exclusive appropriation of land or the products of collective social labor.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Labor Theory Critique**: Systematic refutation of the claim that labor creates property rights
- **Collective Labor Argument**: The products of labor are inherently social, not individual

## Key Concepts
- **Labor as Value Source**: The claim that labor is the source of economic value
- **Land Appropriation**: The claim that mixing labor with land creates property rights
- **Universal Consent**: The claim that property is justified by society's collective agreement
- **Prescription**: The claim that long-term possession creates legitimate title

## Mental Models
- **Use social analysis to counter individual claims**: Think of economic production as inherently collective, not individual
- **Land as common heritage**: Prefer understanding land as a natural resource that cannot be legitimately appropriated by individuals

## Anti-patterns
- **Individualistic labor theory**: Treating economic production as if it were solely the result of individual effort while ignoring social contributions
- **Ignoring social context**: Analyzing labor and property without considering the collective conditions that make production possible

## Key Takeaways
1. Labor cannot create a legitimate title to land because land is not the product of labor
2. The products of labor are inherently social, incorporating contributions from society as a whole
3. Universal consent does not exist and cannot justify property institutions
4. Prescription (long-term possession) does not create moral rights; it merely sanctions existing appropriation
5. Labor creates value, but not the right to exclusively appropriate that value
6. The collective nature of production undermines any individual claim to property
