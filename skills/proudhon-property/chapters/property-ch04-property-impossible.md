# What Is Property? - Chapter 4: That Property Is Impossible

## Core Idea
Proudhon delivers his comprehensive refutation of property as a legitimate institution, demonstrating that it cannot be justified on any moral, logical, or economic grounds. Property is revealed as theft: the appropriation of collective social value by individuals.

## Frameworks Introduated
- **Comprehensive Refutation**: The culmination of Proudhon's dialectical demolition of all property justifications
- **Property as Theft**: The central thesis that property represents the appropriation of society's collective product

## Key Concepts
- **Theft**: The taking of what rightfully belongs to others; applied to property as the taking of social value
- **Collective Product**: The combined result of society's labor, knowledge, and institutions that enable all production
- **Impossibility of Justification**: No coherent argument can justify property's exclusive appropriation
- **Property as Fiction**: Property is a legal fiction maintained by force and convention, not a natural or moral reality

## Mental Models
- **Use collective analysis to expose individual claims**: Think of all wealth as the product of social co-operation, not individual effort
- **Property as ongoing theft**: Prefer understanding property not as a one-time appropriation but as continuous extraction of social value

## Anti-patterns
- **Individual desert**: The belief that individuals deserve their property because of their efforts, ignoring the social context that makes those efforts possible
- **Legal legitimacy**: The belief that legal recognition makes property morally justified

## Key Takeaways
1. Property is theft: it appropriates the collective product of society
2. No moral, logical, or economic justification for property can be sustained
3. All property represents the appropriation of social value by individuals
4. Property is maintained by force and legal fiction, not by justice or reason
5. The impossibility of justifying property demonstrates the need for alternative economic arrangements
