# What Is Property? - Chapter 5: Psychological Exposition of the Idea of Justice

## Core Idea
Proudhon develops his concept of justice as a synthetic principle that resolves contradictions rather than a compromise between opposing interests. True justice recognizes and satisfies all legitimate claims in a social situation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Justice as Synthesis**: Proudhon's concept of justice as the resolution of contradictions rather than their suppression
- **Equality as Foundation**: The principle that justice requires equal recognition of all legitimate interests

## Key Concepts
- **Synthetic Principle**: Justice resolves contradictions by identifying and satisfying the legitimate elements on both sides
- **Equality**: The fundamental requirement that all individuals have equal rights and dignity
- **Reciprocity**: The mutual recognition of rights and obligations between individuals
- **Balance**: The equilibrium of interests that results from just resolutions

## Mental Models
- **Use synthesis to resolve conflicts**: Think of justice as creating solutions that satisfy all legitimate claims rather than balancing competing interests
- **Equality as baseline**: Prefer starting from a presumption of equality rather than hierarchy in social relations

## Anti-patterns
- **Compromise as justice**: Treating justice as a bargaining between unequal parties rather than a recognition of equal rights
- **Might makes right**: Justifying unequal outcomes based on power differentials rather than moral principles

## Key Takeaways
1. Justice is not a compromise but a synthesis that satisfies all legitimate claims
2. Equality is the foundation of all just social arrangements
3. Reciprocity requires mutual recognition of rights and obligations
4. True justice creates balance and harmony, not conflict and domination
5. Justice applied to property requires recognizing the collective claims on all wealth
