# Cheatsheet - Property Is Theft!

## Proudhon's Core Argument in 5 Points

1. **Property is Theft**: Property allows individuals to appropriate the collective product of society
2. **Property is Impossible**: No consistent moral, logical, or economic justification for property can be sustained
3. **Contradictions Drive History**: Economic institutions evolve through the resolution of internal contradictions
4. **Mutualism is the Solution**: Economic relations should be based on reciprocity, equivalence, and mutual recognition
5. **Federalism is the Form**: Political organization should flow from the bottom up through voluntary federation

---

## Property Justifications - Refutation Matrix

| Justification | Claim | Proudhon's Refutation | Alternative |
|--------------|-------|---------------------|------------|
| **Natural Right** | Property is a universal, inalienable right | No consistent definition; contradicts other natural rights | Possession as legitimate use |
| **Occupation** | Taking possession creates rights | Cannot create universal rights; arbitrary | Occupancy based on use, not exclusion |
| **Labor** | Mixing labor with land creates rights | Land is not labor's product; social contribution ignored | Individual possession of products of one's labor |
| **Civil Law** | Law validates property | Law sanctions theft; legal ≠ moral | Law reform based on justice |
| **Universal Consent** | Society collectively agrees to property | Consent does not exist; cannot justify | Consensus-based institutions |
| **Prescription** | Long-term possession creates rights | Time does not create morality | Continuous legitimate use |

## Mutualism Key Principles

### ✅ DO:
- Base exchange on **equivalent value** (cost = price)
- Eliminate **interest** and **profit** as exploitation
- Organize production through **worker co-operatives**
- Provide credit through **mutual banks** without interest
- Distribute according to **labor** and **need**
- Organize politically through **federations** of autonomous communes
- Make decisions through **direct democracy** and **recallable delegates**

### ❌ DON'T:
- Accept **property** as legitimate exclusive appropriation
- Charge **interest** on loans (usury)
- Allow **profit** from others' labor (exploitation)
- Concentrate **capital** in the hands of a few
- Create **hierarchy** in political organization
- Rely on **state** authority for regulation
- Treat **labor** as a commodity to be bought and sold

## Mutualism vs. Capitalism vs. State Socialism

| Aspect | Mutualism | Capitalism | State Socialism |
|--------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Property** | Possession (use) | Private property | State property |
| **Exchange** | Equivalent value | Market pricing | Central planning |
| **Production** | Worker co-operatives | Private enterprise | State enterprises |
| **Credit** | Mutual credit, no interest | Banking with interest | State credit |
| **Distribution** | According to labor | According to capital | According to state plan |
| **Governance** | Federal, direct democracy | Representative democracy | Authoritarian |
| **Authority** | Voluntary association | Hierarchical | Centralized |
| **Goal** | Justice through reciprocity | Profit maximization | State control |

```
   │

   │

```

**Key Principles:**
- Power flows **upward** through delegation
- Delegates are **recallable** at any time
- Each level maintains **autonomy**
- Decisions made at **most local level** possible
- **Voluntary** association between levels

## Common Misconceptions

❌ **"Proudhon is against all property"** → ⚠️ He distinguishes between **possession** (legitimate) and **property** (illegitimate exclusive appropriation)

❌ **"Proudhon is a socialist"** → ⚠️ He rejects both capitalism and state socialism, advocating **mutualism** as a third way

❌ **"Proudhon rejects all authority"** → ⚠️ He rejects **hierarchical** authority, but accepts **voluntary**, **specific**, **recallable** delegation

❌ **"Proudhon is utopian"** → ⚠️ He provides concrete institutional proposals (mutual banks, federations) not just abstract ideals

❌ **"Proudhon opposes all organization"** → ⚠️ He opposes **hierarchy**; advocates **federal** organization from the bottom up

❌ **"Proudhon is individualist"** → ⚠️ His individualism is **social** — individuals develop through co-operative relations, not in isolation
