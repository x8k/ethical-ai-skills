## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Theory of Self-Government
**Use when**: Advocating for women's political participation
- **Core principle**: Governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed (quote from Susan B. Anthony on title page)
- **Application**: If women are not allowed to vote, the government lacks moral legitimacy
- **Anti-pattern**: "Protection" as justification for disenfranchisement — protection is the excuse of tyrants

### The Caste of Sex
**Use when**: Analyzing systemic gender oppression
- **Core principle**: Women occupy the position of Pariahs in a caste system where men are the Brahmins
- **Mechanism**: Separate codes of morals, penalties, industries, religions, educational rights, and relations to government for men and women
- **Historical parallel**: Just as caste in India divides society, the caste of sex divides humanity
- **Anti-pattern**: Claiming women's subjection is "ordained of heaven" — used to maintain oppression

### The Progress Principle
**Use when**: Countering arguments that change is premature or dangerous
- **Core principle**: "It is too early to write the history of this movement; wait until our object is attained"
- **Rebuttal**: This is the argument of those who benefit from the status quo; progress cannot wait for perfect conditions
- **Historical evidence**: Every reform (abolition of slavery, religious freedom, etc.) faced the same objection
- **Application**: Women's suffrage must be demanded now, not deferred to some future perfect moment

### The Economic Argument for Equality
**Use when**: Demonstrating how women's lack of rights harms society
- **Core principle**: Women have been the great unpaid laborers of the world
- **Mechanism**: Women's labor enriches others (fathers, husbands, masters) while women remain in poverty
- **Consequence**: This creates a dependent class that cannot contribute fully to civilization
- **Solution**: Equal rights to property, wages, and political participation

### The Religious Objection and Rebuttal
**Use when**: Addressing claims that women's subordination is divinely ordained
- **Core principle**: Religion has been used throughout history to justify oppression
- **Mechanism**: Isolated biblical texts used to block progress (just as with slavery, witchcraft, capital punishment)
- **Rebuttal**: The general principles of religion favor freedom and equality; isolated texts reflect human interpretation, not divine will
- **Historical evidence**: Witchcraft trials, burning of witches, all used religious justification

### The Education Imperative
**Use when**: Advocating for women's access to knowledge
- **Core principle**: "In the scientific education of woman, in the training of her faculties to independent thought and logical reasoning, lies the hope of the future"
- **Mechanism**: Education frees the mind from bondage of authority and makes the individual self-asserting
- **Consequence**: Without education, women remain prey to superstition and accept their own degradation
- **Historical example**: Christine of Pisa, Margaret of Angoulême, Lucrezia Marinella — educated women who achieved greatness

### The Equality of the Sexes
**Use when**: Countering biological determinism arguments
- **Core principle**: Differences between sexes indicate different duties in the same sphere, not separate spheres
- **Evidence**: Women have shown equal capacity in all human duties throughout history
- **Historical examples**: Women have governed nations, led armies, contributed to art, science, invention, literature
- **Anti-pattern**: "Separate spheres" ideology — used to justify exclusion from all male domains

### The Self-Protection Argument
**Use when**: Addressing the claim that women don't need the vote because men will protect them
- **Core principle**: Women need the ballot to hold in their own right hand the weapon of self-protection and self-defense
- **Rebuttal to**: "Man is woman's natural protector"
- **Counter**: If men uniformly robbed women of property rights until 1848, they cannot be trusted with personal rights
- **Evidence**: Court calendars show fathers, husbands, brothers, and sons dealing unjustly with women

### The Social Revolution Fear
**Use when**: Addressing concerns that women's equality will cause social upheaval
- **Core principle**: "The fear of a social revolution thus complicated the discussion"
- **Rebuttal**: Men fear that with political equality, they can no longer keep women in social subordination
- **Historical evidence**: Same fear expressed about abolition of slavery, religious freedom, etc.
- **Reality**: True equality does not cause revolution; it prevents it by removing injustice

### The Delay Tactic
**Use when**: Countering arguments to wait for a "better time"
- **Core principle**: "It is too early to write the history of this movement; wait until our object is attained"
- **Rebuttal**: This is the argument of oppressors throughout history
- **Historical parallel**: Same said about abolition, religious tolerance, democracy
- **Application**: Justice delayed is justice denied; the time for equality is always now
