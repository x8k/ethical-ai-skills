# Chapter 15: Woman, Church, and State

## Core Idea
Religious institutions and theological interpretations have been powerful forces in maintaining women's subordination throughout history. This chapter provides a comprehensive analysis of how Church and State have collaborated to oppress women, and why women's emancipation requires challenging religious authority.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Theological Oppression Matrix
- **Core**: Woman's subordination is maintained through religious doctrine, scriptural interpretation, and ecclesiastical authority
- **Mechanisms**: Original sin teachings, marriage as hierarchy, exclusion from religious offices, selective scriptural interpretation
- **Historical Examples**: Witchcraft trials, marriage laws under various churches, restrictions on women's religious participation
- **Solution**: Reinterpretation of scripture and religious principle to support equality

### The Church-State Nexus
- **Core**: Church and State have historically collaborated to enforce women's subordination
- **Mechanism**: Religious institutions provide moral justification; State provides legal enforcement
- **Result**: A powerful system of control that is difficult to challenge
- **Solution**: Separation of Church and State, religious freedom, and independent interpretation

## Key Concepts
- **Woman under Old Religions**: Historical oppression through religious law and custom
- **Women in Early Christian Church**: Evidence of women's participation in early Christian communities
- **Original Sin**: Theological concept used to justify women's inferiority and subordination
- **Celibacy of the Clergy**: Church policy that reinforced gender hierarchy and control over women
- **Feudalism and Marriage**: Historical systems that treated women as property
- **Witchcraft**: Phenomenon where women were disproportionately accused, tried, and executed
- **Burning of Witches**: Extreme example of religious persecution of women
- **Marriage under Various Churches**: Different religious traditions' treatment of women in marriage
- **Salic Law**: Law excluding women from inheritance and rule
- **Mormon Doctrine**: Religious teachings on women that reinforced subordination
- **Milton's Influence**: John Milton's views on women's subordination and their lasting impact
- **Sunday-School Teachings**: Religious education that reinforced gender stereotypes
- **Ordination Controversies**: Debates over women's ability to serve as religious leaders

## Mental Models
- Use **historical religious analysis** when examining gender oppression: look at how religious institutions have both oppressed and been reformed regarding women
- Think of **religion as a double-edged sword**: while used to justify oppression, religious principles can also support liberation
- Apply **the consistency test to religious claims**: if a religious principle (equality, justice, love) is accepted, it must apply to women
- Use **comparative religious analysis**: examine how different religious traditions treat women to understand the cultural, not divine, basis of subordination

## Anti-patterns
- **Divine Ordination Claims**: Asserting that women's subordination is God's will ignores the historical, cultural basis of religious teachings
- **Selective Scriptural Literalism**: Using isolated biblical texts to justify oppression while ignoring broader principles of equality and justice
- **Religious Deference**: Allowing religious authority to determine secular rights prevents progress and violates separation of church and state
- **Theology of Inferiority**: Teaching that women are inherently spiritually inferior justifies exclusion from religious life

## Key Takeaways
1. Religious institutions have been central to maintaining women's subordination throughout history
2. Theological interpretations, not divine will, have justified women's oppression
3. Women have been active participants in religious life when allowed, demonstrating capability and leadership
4. The same religious principles that justified slavery and other oppressions have been used against women
5. Religious freedom requires the right to interpret scripture and religious principle independently
6. The separation of Church and State is essential for women's secular rights
7. Progressive religious reinterpretation is possible and necessary for women's full equality
