# Cheatsheet — History of Woman Suffrage, Vol. I

## Quick Reference: Core Arguments FOR Women's Rights

| Argument | Key Principle | Best Used Against | Historical Example |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Consent of the Governed** | Governments derive just powers from consent of the governed | Political exclusion | Declaration of Independence (1776) |
| **Natural Rights** | All humans have inherent rights by nature | "Women are different" | All men are created equal |
| **Self-Protection** | Women need rights to protect themselves | "Men will protect women" | Court cases, property disputes |
| **Economic Contribution** | Women's labor benefits society; contributors deserve rights | "Women belong in home" | Unpaid labor, industrial work |
| **Historical Progress** | All oppressed classes gain rights eventually | "It's too early" | Abolition, religious freedom |
| **Religious Principle** | True religion supports equality | "God ordains subordination" | Scriptural reinterpretation |
| **Social Harmony** | Equality creates harmony, oppression causes conflict | "It will cause revolution" | Abolition movement success |

## Quick Reference: Common Objections and Rebuttals

| Objection | Rebuttal | Supporting Evidence |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| **"Women don't need the vote; men protect them"** | Protection is the excuse of tyrants; women need self-protection | Court records show women unjustly treated by male relatives |
| **"Women are naturally inferior"** | Women have proven capability in all fields throughout history | Queens, military leaders, scientists, writers, business owners |
| **"It's too early for women's rights"** | Every reform faced this argument; justice delayed is justice denied | Abolition, religious tolerance, democracy movements |
| **"God ordains women's subordination"** | Religious principles favor equality; isolated texts reflect human interpretation | Witchcraft trials, slavery justification |
| **"Women's equality will cause social upheaval"** | Oppression causes conflict; equality creates harmony | Successful reforms in other areas |
| **"Women can't compete with men"** | Competition benefits society; exclusion wastes talent | Economic contributions, professional achievements |
| **"Women belong in the domestic sphere"** | Differences indicate different duties in the same sphere, not separation | Historical achievements in all spheres |

## Key Historical Events Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **1840** | World's Anti-Slavery Convention, London | Women delegates (including Stanton, Mott) excluded; catalyzes women's rights movement |
| **1848** | Seneca Falls Convention, NY | First women's rights convention; Declaration of Sentiments adopted |
| **1848** | Married Women's Property Act, NY | First law recognizing married women's property rights |
| **1850** | Salem Women's Rights Convention, OH | Early Ohio organizing |
| **1850** | Medical College for Women opens, PA | First women's medical college |
| **1851** | Akron Women's Rights Convention, OH | Continued Ohio organizing |
| **1852** | Massachusetts Women's Rights Convention | State-level organizing |
| **1852** | New York State Temperance Convention | Women organize for temperance |
| **1853** | Cleveland Women's Rights Convention, OH | Continued Ohio momentum |
| **1853** | Mob in Broadway Tabernacle, NY | Opposition to women's rights convention |
| **1853** | Pennsylvania Constitution restricts women's voting | Setback for women's political rights |
| **1854** | Albany Convention, NY | State legislative hearings on suffrage |
| **1854** | Ohio Women's Suffrage Association formed | Formal state organization |
| **1855** | Cincinnati Women's Rights Convention, OH | Continued organizing |
| **1859** | Constitutional Convention, Kansas | Women present petitions for suffrage |
| **1860** | Married Women's Property Law, NY | Further expansion of property rights |

## Argument Framework: How to Build a Women's Rights Case

### Step 1: Establish Shared Principles
- Start with principles opponent accepts (equality, justice, democracy, Christianity)
- Show these principles apply to women

### Step 2: Present Evidence of Oppression
- Historical examples of women's exclusion
- Current legal inequalities
- Economic and social harms from inequality

### Step 3: Answer Objections
- Use the objection-rebuttal pairs above
- Cite historical parallels (slavery, religious persecution)
- Show the benefits of equality

### Step 4: Call for Action
- Specific legislative changes needed
- Personal commitment to support women's rights
- Vision of a more just society
