# Section IV: Argument from Justice

## Core Idea
The claim for women's equality rests on fundamental principles of justice already accepted in democratic societies. It is not a radical new demand but the logical application of existing principles to half of humanity.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Democratic Consistency**: If democratic principles are accepted (all men are created equal, government by consent of the governed), then excluding women is a contradiction.
- **Taxation and Representation**: A fundamental principle that those who pay taxes must have representation — a principle that applies to women.
- **Universal Rights Logic**: Rights are not privileges granted by the powerful but inherent entitlements that apply to all humans.

## Key Concepts
- **Equal Creation**: The Declaration of Independence's principle that "all men are created equal" — where "men" means human beings, not males only.
- **Inalienable Rights**: Life, liberty, and pursuit of happiness are rights of all, not just half of humanity.
- **Consent of the Governed**: Just government requires the consent of all who are governed, not just half.
- **Jury of Peers**: Women are tried by male judges and juries, violating the principle that all should be tried by their peers.

## Mental Models
- **Principles over precedent**: Existing principles should guide reform, not existing practices.
- **Inclusion as consistency**: Applying democratic principles to women makes democracy truly universal.
- **Justice as equality**: Equal treatment under the law is the definition of justice.

## Anti-patterns
- **Selective application**: Applying democratic principles to men but not women.
- **Dishonest interpretation**: Claiming "men" in founding documents excludes women when it clearly means humans.
- **Tradition as justification**: Using "it's always been this way" to justify injustice.

## Key Takeaways
1. Women's rights claims are based on existing democratic principles, not new radical ideas.
2. The Declaration of Independence's principles logically extend to women.
3. Taxation without representation is as unjust for women as it was for colonial Americans.
4. Equal protection under the law requires equal political rights.
5. Justice demands that no degrading distinctions be made without necessity.
