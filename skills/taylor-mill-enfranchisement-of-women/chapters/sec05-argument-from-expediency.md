# Section V: Argument from Expediency

## Core Idea
Beyond moral justice, women's enfranchisement is practically beneficial. Including women's talents, perspectives, and labor improves governance, economic efficiency, and social harmony.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Talent Utilization**: Society benefits from using all available talents, regardless of sex.
- **Diversity of Perspective**: Women's different life experiences provide valuable insights for governance.
- **Economic Efficiency**: Removing artificial restrictions on half the population increases productivity.
- **Social Harmony**: Equality reduces resentment and conflict between sexes.

## Key Concepts
- **Wasted Potential**: Excluding women from professions and public life means society loses their contributions.
- **Competition Benefits**: While some worry about increased competition, the overall benefit of including women outweighs concerns.
- **Governance Improvement**: Women's participation in government would improve laws and administration.
- **Social Stability**: Equal rights reduce the "perversion and demoralization" caused by caste-like divisions between sexes.

## Mental Models
- **Whole society benefit**: Women's equality isn't just good for women; it benefits all of society.
- **Resources as capabilities**: Human talents are resources that should be fully utilized.
- **Conflict reduction**: Equality eliminates the resentment that comes from arbitrary exclusion.

## Anti-patterns
- **Zero-sum thinking**: Viewing women's gains as men's losses rather than society's gains.
- **Fear of change**: Opposing equality based on unfounded concerns about disruption.
- **Short-term focus**: Sacrificing long-term benefits for short-term comfort with existing arrangements.

## Key Takeaways
1. Including women's talents benefits all of society, not just women.
2. Artificial restrictions on women reduce overall productivity and progress.
3. Women's political participation would improve the quality of governance.
4. Equality between sexes reduces social conflict and demoralization.
5. The practical benefits of enfranchisement are as compelling as the moral arguments.
