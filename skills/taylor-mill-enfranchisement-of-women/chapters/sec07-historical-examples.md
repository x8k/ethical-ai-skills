# Section VII: Historical Examples and Precedents

## Core Idea
History provides numerous examples of women in positions of power and responsibility, demonstrating their capability for public life. The subjection of women, like other injustices, has been overcome when societies recognized its inconsistency with their principles.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Historical Capability Evidence**: Women have demonstrated fitness for governance throughout history when given the opportunity.
- **Precedent for Change**: Every expansion of rights (end of slavery, religious tolerance) was once considered radical but is now accepted.
- **Power and Virtue Analysis**: The powerful define virtue for the powerless to maintain their dominance.

## Key Concepts

### Women in Power
- **Queens and Regents**: Elizabeth I, Isabella of Castile, Maria Theresa, Catherine of Russia, Blanche of Castile — all demonstrated exceptional ruling capability.
- **Diplomats and Advisers**: Women in royal houses have equaled the best statesmen of their time as diplomats and governors.
- **Military Leaders**: Chatelaines like Jeanne de Montfort and the Countess of Derby distinguished themselves militarily.
- **Administrators**: Women in semi-barbarous Asia, despite seclusion, provided excellent examples of administration.

### Evolution of Domestic Relations
- **Slave Stage**: Women as slaves for physical labor (e.g., Australian savages, American Indians).
- **Sensuality Stage**: Women as slaves for men's pleasure (e.g., Asia).
- **Companionship Stage**: Women as protected dependents, with duties of kindness and loyalty owed by men.
- **Modern Stage**: Increasing sense of reciprocal obligation, though the dependent condition remains.

### Historical Parallels
- **Negro Slavery**: Like women's subjection, was once considered natural and necessary. Abolitionists are now strong, showing change is possible.
- **Monarchical Despotism**: Overthrown through democratic revolutions.
- **Feudal Nobility**: Abolished as societies recognized the injustice of inherited privilege.
- **Religious Disabilities**: Overcome as societies recognized the principle of religious freedom.

## Mental Models
- **History as proof**: Past examples demonstrate capability and show that what was once radical can become accepted.
- **Power reveals capability**: When women have had power, they've demonstrated the same range of abilities as men.
- **Injustice as temporary**: Every form of subjection has eventually been recognized as unjust and abolished.

## Anti-patterns
- **Exceptionalism**: Dismissing historical examples as exceptions rather than evidence of general capability.
- **Cultural relativism**: Accepting that what's normal in a culture is therefore just.
- **Selective memory**: Remembering women's failures but forgetting their successes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Historical examples demonstrate women's capability for all forms of public life.
2. Women have successfully governed, administered, and led in various contexts throughout history.
3. The subjection of women follows the same pattern as other forms of oppression that have been overcome.
4. Every expansion of rights was once considered impossible but is now accepted.
5. Modern sensibilities recognize the injustice of women's subjection, just as they recognize the injustice of slavery.
6. Domestic relations have evolved from slavery to companionship, showing capacity for further progress.
