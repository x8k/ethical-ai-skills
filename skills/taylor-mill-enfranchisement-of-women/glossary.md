# Glossary — The Enfranchisement of Women

*Knowledge base from "The Enfranchisement of Women" by Harriet Taylor Mill (1851)*

---

## A
- **Argument from Expediency** — Practical benefits of women's enfranchisement: improved governance, economic efficiency, social harmony (sec05)
- **Argument from Justice** — Moral case for women's rights based on democratic principles: equal rights, taxation and representation, consent of the governed (sec04)

## C
- **Character Hardening Objection** — The claim that public life would make women harsh and unfeeling; refuted as outdated stereotype (sec06)
- **Co-equal Share** — Equal participation in forming and administering laws at all levels (sec03)
- **Competition Objection** — The claim that women's economic participation would depress wages; refuted as outweighed by benefits (sec06)
- **Consent of the Governed** — Democratic principle that government requires the consent of all who are governed, not just half (sec04)

## D
- **Democratic Consistency** — Applying democratic principles (all men are created equal) to women as well as men (sec04)
- **Dependent Condition** — Women's historical status as legally and economically subordinate to men (sec07)
- **Domestic Power Dynamics** — The evolution of marriage from women as slaves to companions, with increasing reciprocal obligations (sec07)

## E
- **Economic Efficiency** — Removing artificial restrictions on half the population increases overall productivity (sec05)
- **Equal Creation** — The Declaration of Independence's principle that "all men are created equal" includes women (sec04)
- **Enfranchisement** — The granting of political rights, particularly suffrage, to women (All sections)
- **Expediency** — Practical usefulness or advantage; argument that women's rights benefit society (sec05)

## F
- **False Compromise** — Accepting partial rights as sufficient rather than demanding full equality (sec08, Anti-pattern)

## H
- **Historical Capability Evidence** — Women's demonstrated fitness for governance throughout history when given opportunity (sec07)
- **Historical Parallels** — Comparison with other rights expansions (abolition of slavery, religious tolerance) that were once radical but are now accepted (sec07)

## I
- **Inalienable Rights** — Life, liberty, and pursuit of happiness as rights of all humans, not just men (sec04)
- **Inherent Right** — Women's rights as inherent entitlements, not privileges to be granted (sec08)

## J
- **Justice** — The moral principle that equal treatment under the law is a fundamental right (sec04)
- **Jury of Peers** — The principle that all should be tried by their peers; violated when women are tried by male judges and juries (sec04)

## M
- **Maternity Objection** — The claim that maternity makes women unfit for public life; refuted as individually applicable, not universal (sec06)

## P
- **Partnership** — Equal sharing in the labors, gains, risks, and remunerations of productive industry (sec03)
- **Political Equality** — Equal rights to vote, hold office, and participate in government (sec02, sec04)
- **Power and Virtue Analysis** — The powerful define virtues for the powerless to serve their interests; male vs. female virtues reflect this (sec07)
- **Precedent for Change** — Every expansion of rights was once considered radical but is now accepted (sec07)
- **Principled Advocacy** — Taking a stand on principles without time-serving or compromise (sec02)
- **Progress Barrier** — Women's subjection as an obstacle to vital social improvement (sec08)

## R
- **Reciprocal Obligation** — The increasing sense of duty that the powerful owe to those under their protection (sec07)
- **Resolutions** — The formal statements of principle adopted by the women's rights convention (sec02)

## S
- **Self-Representation Principle** — Women as active agents demanding their own rights, not passive beneficiaries (sec01)
- **Social Harmony** — The reduction of conflict and resentment between sexes through equality (sec05)
- **Social Progress** — The broader movement toward greater justice and equality, of which women's enfranchisement is part (sec08)
- **Social Stability** — The reduction of "perversion and demoralization" caused by caste-like divisions between sexes (sec05)
- **Structural Equality** — Equal rights requiring structural changes in education, economics, and politics (sec03)
- **Suffrage** — The right to vote; claimed as a right, not requested as a favor (sec02, sec04)

## T
- **Taxation and Representation** — The principle that those who pay taxes must have political representation (sec04)
- **Talent Utilization** — Society benefits from using all available talents, regardless of sex (sec05)

## U
- **Universal Application** — Rights claims apply to all women, not just educated or propertied women (sec02)
- **Universal Rights Logic** — Rights are inherent entitlements that apply to all humans (sec04)

## W
- **Women's Rights Convention** — Organized gatherings where women demanded political, civil, and social equality (sec01)
- **Wasted Potential** — The loss to society when women are excluded from professions and public life (sec05)
