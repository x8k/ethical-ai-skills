## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Reason as the Foundation of Rights
**Use when**: Establishing the basis for women's equality and human dignity
- **Core insight**: If reason is the defining human characteristic, and women share this faculty with men, then women are entitled to the same rights as men
- **Enlightenment foundation**: Wollstonecraft applies Enlightenment principles of reason and universality to gender
- **Implication**: Denying women rights is to deny their humanity and the reason they possess
- **Application**: Any restriction on women's education, participation, or rights must be justified by demonstrating women's lack of reason — which cannot be done

### Education as Liberation
**Use when**: Analyzing the role of education in social equality and personal development
- **Core insight**: Education develops the faculty of reason, enabling individuals to think for themselves and contribute to society
- **Wollstonecraft's argument**: Women are kept in a state of ignorance and dependence through lack of education
- **Purpose of education**: To enable individuals to fulfill their duties to themselves, their families, and society
- **Application**: Education must be available to all, regardless of gender, to develop virtue and capability

### Virtue as the Same for All
**Use when**: Establishing moral equality between genders
- **Core insight**: Virtue is based on reason and the fulfillment of duty, not on gender-specific behaviors
- **Wollstonecraft's critique**: The double standard that values different virtues in men and women serves to keep women subordinate
- **True virtue**: For both men and women, virtue consists in the rational pursuit of duty and the development of character
- **Application**: Moral education and expectations should be the same for all, based on reason, not gender

### Critique of False Refinement
**Use when**: Analyzing how social norms degrade women's character
- **Core insight**: The "refinement" praised in women is actually a form of corruption that prioritizes appearance over substance
- **Effect on women**: Women are educated to be pleasing objects rather than rational, moral beings
- **Effect on men**: Men are corrupted by treating women as inferior beings to be controlled and possessed
- **Application**: True refinement consists in the development of reason and virtue, not in superficial accomplishments

### Marriage as Friendship
**Use when**: Reimagining marital relationships based on equality and mutual respect
- **Core insight**: Marriage should be a partnership of equals based on mutual respect and shared values, not a relationship of domination
- **Wollstonecraft's ideal**: Marriage as a friendship between rational beings who choose each other freely
- **Contrast with reality**: Current marriage practices treat women as property and deny their agency
- **Application**: Marital reform requires women's education, economic independence, and legal equality

### Rights and Duties Reciprocity
**Use when**: Understanding the relationship between rights and responsibilities
- **Core insight**: Rights and duties are reciprocal; one cannot have rights without fulfilling duties, and duties cannot be fulfilled without rights
- **Wollstonecraft's application**: Women's lack of rights prevents them from fulfilling their duties to society and family
- **Social benefit**: Enabling women to fulfill their duties benefits all of society through improved family life, education of children, and moral improvement
- **Application**: Extending rights to women is not just for their benefit but for the benefit of all society

### National Education
**Use when**: Designing educational systems that serve social needs
- **Core insight**: Education should be designed to create virtuous citizens who can contribute to the common good
- **Wollstonecraft's proposals**: National education system that includes both men and women, teaching reason, virtue, and useful skills
- **Public benefit**: Educated women will be better mothers, wives, and citizens
- **Application**: Education as a public good requiring public support and universal access

### Critique of Rousseau
**Use when**: Analyzing the contradictions in Enlightenment thinkers' views on women
- **Core insight**: Even progressive thinkers like Rousseau can hold contradictory and oppressive views on women
- **Rousseau's error**: His educational theory (Emile) advocates freedom and reason for men (Emile) but subordination for women (Sophie)
- **Wollstonecraft's refutation**: The same principles that justify men's education and rights apply equally to women
- **Application**: Enlightenment ideals must be consistently applied to all humans, not just to men

### Women as Part of the Human Species
**Use when**: Countering arguments that women are fundamentally different from men
- **Core insight**: Women are human beings first, with the same rational faculty and moral capacity as men
- **Wollstonecraft's argument**: Differences between men and women are the result of education and socialization, not nature
- **Implication**: Women should be judged by the same standards as men, with the same opportunities and expectations
- **Application**: Any distinction between men and women must be justified by reason, not tradition or prejudice
