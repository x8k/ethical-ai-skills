# Chapter 2: The Prevailing Opinion of a Sexual Character Discussed

## Core Idea
Wollstonecraft critiques the prevailing belief that women have a distinct "sexual character" that makes them fundamentally different from men. She argues that the supposed differences are the result of education and socialization, not nature, and that the same standards of reason and virtue should apply to both genders.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Gender as Social Construct**: The argument that differences between men and women are created by society, not inherent in nature
- **Double Standard of Reason**: The critique that men value reason in themselves but not in women
- **Sensibility vs. Reason**: The false opposition between emotional sensibility (valued in women) and rational thought (valued in men)

## Key Concepts
- **Sexual Character**: The supposed inherent, gender-specific traits that distinguish men from women
- **Masculine Reason**: The association of rationality with men and masculinity
- **Feminine Sensibility**: The association of emotionality and delicacy with women and femininity
- **Cultivated Weakness**: Women's dependence and delicacy as the result of their education, not their nature
- **Natural Equality**: The underlying equality of men and women in terms of rational capacity
- **Artificial Distinctions**: Socially created differences that serve to maintain hierarchy

## Mental Models
- **Use reason to counter stereotypes**: Think of gender differences as socially constructed and subject to change through education
- **Sensibility and reason as complementary**: Prefer understanding emotion and reason as both necessary human faculties, not as gendered opposites
- **Education creates character**: Recognize that supposed gender characteristics are the result of differential education

## Anti-patterns
- **Biological determinism**: The belief that gender differences are inherent and unchangeable
- **Complementarianism**: The belief that men and women have different but complementary roles that justify hierarchy
- **Essentialism**: The belief in inherent, unchangeable gender characteristics

## Key Takeaways
1. The supposed "sexual character" of women is the product of education and socialization, not nature
2. Men value reason in themselves but discourage it in women, preferring sensibility and delicacy
3. The opposition between reason (masculine) and sensibility (feminine) is a false dichotomy
4. Women's cultivated weakness and dependence serve the interests of men, not women
5. True equality requires that the same standards of reason and virtue apply to both genders
6. Gender differences are artificial distinctions maintained by unequal education
7. Wollstonecraft anticipates modern feminist critiques of gender as social construct
