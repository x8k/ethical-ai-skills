# Chapter 6: Of the Pernicious Effects which Arise from the Unnatural Distinctions Established in Society

## Core Idea
Wollstonecraft analyzes how artificial social distinctions, particularly those of class and gender, create harm by reinforcing hierarchy, encouraging vice, and preventing the development of virtue and reason in all members of society.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Unnatural Distinctions**: Socially created hierarchies that have no basis in nature or reason
- **Hierarchy as Corruption**: The argument that systems of rank and privilege corrupt both the powerful and the powerless
- **Artificial Inequality**: Inequalities created by social institutions rather than by natural differences

## Key Concepts
- **Class Distinctions**: Hierarchies based on birth, wealth, or social status
- **Gender Distinctions**: Hierarchies based on sex that place women below men
- **Artificial Hierarchy**: Systems of rank that serve to maintain power rather than to reflect merit
- **Corruption of the Powerful**: How privilege leads to vice in those who possess it
- **Degradation of the Powerless**: How subordination prevents the development of virtue in those without power
- **Social Harm**: The damage done to society by maintaining artificial distinctions
- **Virtue Suppression**: How hierarchy prevents the development of moral character in all

## Mental Models
- **Use equality to prevent corruption**: Think of social distinctions as creating vices in both the privileged and the oppressed
- **Artificial vs. natural**: Recognize that social hierarchies are constructed, not inevitable or natural
- **Systemic corruption**: Understand that inequality corrupts the entire social body, not just individuals

## Anti-patterns
- **Natural hierarchy**: The belief that social inequalities reflect natural or divinely ordained differences
- **Meritocratic hierarchy**: The belief that existing hierarchies reward merit rather than reinforcing privilege
- **Benign distinctions**: Treating social hierarchies as harmless or beneficial

## Key Takeaways
1. Artificial distinctions of class and gender create social harm by reinforcing hierarchy
2. These distinctions corrupt both the powerful (through vice) and the powerless (through degraded character)
3. Class hierarchies serve to maintain power and privilege, not to reflect natural differences
4. Gender hierarchies place women below men, preventing both from developing full virtue
5. Artificial inequality prevents the development of reason and moral character in all members of society
6. The same principles that justify the abolition of class distinctions apply to gender distinctions
7. True social improvement requires eliminating artificial hierarchies and establishing equality
