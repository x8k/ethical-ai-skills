# Chapter 7: On National Education

## Core Idea
Wollstonecraft presents her proposals for a national system of education that would be universally available and designed to create virtuous, rational citizens. She argues that education is a public good that benefits all of society and should be publicly supported.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Public Education**: Education as a social responsibility and public good
- **Civic Virtue**: The development of character and capability that enables citizens to contribute to the common good
- **Universal Access**: Education available to all, regardless of gender or class

## Key Concepts
- **National Education System**: A publicly funded and administered system of schools
- **Virtuous Citizens**: Individuals educated to develop reason, moral character, and social responsibility
- **Useful Knowledge**: Practical skills and information that enable individuals to contribute to society
- **Moral Instruction**: Education in ethics, duty, and character development
- **Intellectual Development**: The cultivation of reasoning abilities through education
- **Public Benefit**: The social advantages of universal education for all citizens

## Mental Models
- **Use public good framework for education**: Think of education as a social investment that benefits all of society
- **Education as civic preparation**: Prefer understanding education as preparation for responsible citizenship
- **Universal access as right**: Recognize that education should be available to all as a matter of justice

## Anti-patterns
- **Private education**: Treating education as a private good available only to those who can pay
- **Gendered curricula**: Providing different education to boys and girls based on supposed natural differences
- **Elite education**: Focusing educational resources on the privileged rather than on universal access

## Key Takeaways
1. A national education system should provide universal access to education for all citizens
2. Education should develop virtuous citizens capable of contributing to the common good
3. Both boys and girls should receive education that develops their reason and moral character
4. Education should include both moral instruction and practical, useful knowledge
5. Public support for education is justified by the public benefits it creates
6. Universal education will improve family life, social morals, and national prosperity
7. Wollstonecraft anticipates modern arguments for public education as a social good
