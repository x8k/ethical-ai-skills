# Glossary - A Vindication of the Rights of Woman

**Accomplishments** — Superficial skills (music, dancing, languages) taught to women as a substitute for reason and virtue; criticized by Wollstonecraft as creating cultivated ignorance (Ch 03)

**Artificial Character** — Personality traits developed in women through their education, not their nature; serves men's interests, not women's (Ch 03)

**Artificial Distinctions** — Socially created hierarchies (class, gender) with no basis in nature or reason; maintained to preserve power and privilege (Ch 06)

**Chastity** — Sexual purity; virtue emphasized in women often to the exclusion of other moral qualities; Wollstonecraft argues true chastity results from rational self-control, not social coercion (Ch 05)

**Civic Virtue** — Character and capability that enable citizens to contribute to the common good; developed through education (Ch 07)

**Cultivated Ignorance** — Women's lack of knowledge and critical thinking as a result of their education; keeps them dependent on men (Ch 03)

**Cultivated Weakness** — Women's dependence and delicacy as the result of their education, not their nature; enables domestic tyranny (Ch 02)

**Dependence** — Women's reliance on men for support and guidance as a result of their lack of education and economic opportunities (Ch 04)

**Domestic Tyranny** — Arbitrary power exercised by men over women within the family, enabled by women's dependence and lack of education (Ch 03, Ch 04)

**Duty** — Obligation to self, family, and society; Wollstonecraft argues women cannot fulfill their duties without education and rights (Ch 01, Ch 04)

**Duty to Self** — Women's obligation to develop their own capabilities and character through education (Ch 01)

**Duty to Society** — Women's obligation to contribute to the common good, which requires education and opportunity (Ch 01)

**Education as Liberation** — Education as the means by which women can achieve equality and fulfill their duties; central framework of Wollstonecraft's argument (Ch 01)

**False Refinement** — Superficial accomplishments and manners that pass for education among women; criticized as undermining true virtue (Ch 03)

**False System of Education** — The education of women that emphasizes appearance and accomplishments over reason and virtue; criticized throughout (Ch 01)

**Feminine Sensibility** — The association of emotionality and delicacy with women and femininity; contrasted with masculine reason (Ch 02)

**Female Revolution** — The transformation of women's social role and character through education and equality; Wollstonecraft's vision (Ch 09)

**Folly of Ignorance** — The ridiculous and harmful behaviors that result from women's lack of reason and education (Ch 08)

**Human Reason** — The faculty that distinguishes humans from animals and forms the basis of rights; shared equally by men and women (Ch 01)

**Ignorance as Social Harm** — The argument that women's lack of education creates problems affecting all of society (Ch 08)

**Moral Development** — The development of genuine character through reason and self-control; applies equally to men and women (Ch 05)

**Moral Improvement** — The development of virtue and reason in women through education and equality; would benefit all of society (Ch 09)

**Moral Philosophy** — The study of virtue and duty; Wollstonecraft argues moral standards should be the same for all (Ch 05)

**Masculine Reason** — The association of rationality with men and masculinity; Wollstonecraft rejects this gendered division (Ch 02)

**Marriage as Property** — The treatment of women as property transferred from father to husband in marriage; criticized as undermining women's agency (Ch 04)

**National Education** — Publicly funded and administered system of schools providing universal access to education for all citizens (Ch 07)

**Natural Equality** — The underlying equality of men and women in terms of rational capacity; basis of Wollstonecraft's argument (Ch 02)

**Pernicious Effects** — The social harms resulting from artificial distinctions of class and gender (Ch 06)

**Public Benefit** — The social advantages of universal education for all citizens; justifies public support (Ch 07)

**Public Happiness** — The common good that results from all individuals fulfilling their duties; goal of Wollstonecraft's educational proposals (Ch 01, Ch 09)

**Reason as Foundation of Rights** — Wollstonecraft's application of Enlightenment principles to gender equality; if women share reason with men, they share the same rights (Ch 01)

**Reform Urgency** — The need for immediate educational reform to prevent the folly and harm resulting from women's ignorance (Ch 08)

**Reputation vs. Virtue** — The distinction between social appearance and true moral character; Wollstonecraft argues emphasis on reputation undermines genuine virtue (Ch 05)

**Revolution in Female Manners** — A fundamental change in women's character, behavior, and social position; would transform society (Ch 09)

**Sexual Character** — The supposed inherent, gender-specific traits that distinguish men from women; Wollstonecraft argues these are socially constructed, not natural (Ch 02)

**Sexual Double Standard** — The different moral standards applied to men's and women's sexual behavior; corrupts both genders (Ch 05)

**Social Expectations** — Norms that pressure women to conform to gendered behaviors; maintain women's subordination (Ch 04)

**Social Folly** — Ridiculous or harmful social practices resulting from women's ignorance and dependence (Ch 08)

**Social Harm** — The damage done to society by maintaining artificial distinctions and women's degradation (Ch 06)

**Sensibility vs. Reason** — The false opposition between emotional sensibility (valued in women) and rational thought (valued in men); both are necessary human faculties (Ch 02, Ch 03)

**Separate Spheres** — The belief that men and women have fundamentally different natures and roles that justify different rights and education; rejected by Wollstonecraft (Ch 01)

**Unnatural Distinctions** — Socially created hierarchies that have no basis in nature or reason; create corruption and prevent virtue (Ch 06)

**Universal Access** — Education available to all, regardless of gender or class; principle of Wollstonecraft's educational proposals (Ch 07)

**Useful Knowledge** — Practical skills and information that enable individuals to contribute to society; component of education (Ch 07)

**Virtue** — Moral character based on reason and the fulfillment of duty; for Wollstonecraft, virtue is the same for all, based on reason, not gender (Ch 01, Ch 05, Ch 09)

**Virtue as the Same for All** — Wollstonecraft's argument that virtue is based on reason and duty, not on gender-specific behaviors; double standards undermine true morality (Ch 03)

**Virtuous Citizens** — Individuals educated to develop reason, moral character, and social responsibility; product of national education (Ch 07)

**Virtuous Society** — A community characterized by reason, moral character, and social responsibility; result of women's moral improvement (Ch 09)

**Women's Duties** — Women's obligations to themselves, their families, and society; require education and rights for fulfillment (Ch 01, Ch 04, Ch 07)

**Women's Ignorance** — Women's lack of knowledge and reasoning ability as a result of their education; creates social harm (Ch 08)

**Women's Nature** — The supposed inherent characteristics of women; Wollstonecraft argues differences are the result of education, not nature (Ch 02, Ch 03, Ch 06)

**Women as Part of the Human Species** — Wollstonecraft's argument that women are human beings first, with the same rational faculty and moral capacity as men (Ch 01)
